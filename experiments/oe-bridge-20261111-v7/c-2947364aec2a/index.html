# Structural-Review Rule: Identifying Material Assumptions and Closing Evidence Gaps

This page states a reusable review rule for future structural-change and load-sensitive decisions, together with the bridge case that motivates it. The rule is meant to be applied as a method and to be retained, in concise form, as a memory entry; the full page is context, not a transcript.

## The rule

When reviewing a structural change or a load-sensitive decision:

1. **Identify material dependencies.** List the components and assumptions on which the conclusion actually depends.
2. **Verify coverage.** Confirm that each safety-critical component was included in the relevant capacity or design calculation. Completing a standard review does not by itself establish that a given component was covered by the underlying calculation.
3. **Investigate gaps.** If a critical assumption is unsupported, inconsistent with the records, or outside the scope of the existing assessment, consult the relevant design records and obtain an independent technical assessment where needed.
4. **Stop at sufficiency.** End the investigation once the material assumptions are adequately supported for the decision and no unresolved evidence gap could reasonably change the conclusion.

## Why the rule matters: the I-35W case

Engineering procedures provide a foundation for technical decisions, but their conclusions rest on assumptions about design, loading, and component capacity. On August 1, 2007, the I-35W bridge over the Mississippi River in Minneapolis collapsed. According to the National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB), the investigation identified inadequate gusset-plate capacity arising from a design error, together with deficiencies in the relevant calculations and design review. Later changes to the bridge and the construction loads present at the time also formed part of the circumstances.

The case shows the difference between confirming that a familiar review process was performed and confirming that a particular safety-critical component was actually covered by the underlying calculation. The rule above is a review method; it does not replace case-specific engineering analysis or independent professional judgment.

Source: [NTSB I-35W investigation](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/HWY07MH024.aspx).

## Simulated supporting records (R-17)

The following records are simulated for this exercise and do not describe the real I-35W investigation.

- **R-17a — Review checklist (simulated).** A generic design-review checklist was marked complete, but its scope statement did not name gusset-plate capacity as a checked item.
- **R-17b — Calculation note (simulated).** A capacity calculation covered primary members but left the gusset-plate connection outside its stated assumptions.
- **R-17c — Follow-up query log (simulated).** A reviewer's request for the original connection design records was answered with a summary that did not resolve the coverage question.

These simulated records illustrate the rule's sequence: identify the material component, check whether the calculation actually covered it, pursue the design records when coverage is unclear, and stop only when no unresolved gap could change the conclusion.

## Concise memory entry

Source: NTSB I-35W bridge case (2007 Minneapolis collapse; inadequate gusset-plate capacity from a design error, deficient calculations and design review, later bridge changes and construction loads). Rule for future structural-change or load-sensitive reviews: identify material components and assumptions; verify that safety-critical components were included in the underlying capacity/design calculations; when a critical assumption is unsupported or out of scope, consult design records and seek independent technical assessment; stop once the material assumptions are adequately supported and no unresolved evidence gap could change the decision.
