# I-35W bridge: checking the calculation behind a review

## What the investigation established

The I-35W highway bridge in Minneapolis collapsed in 2007. The U.S. National Transportation Safety Board found that gusset plates at the U10 nodes had inadequate load capacity because of a design error. Previous modifications had increased the bridge's weight; traffic and concentrated construction loads were present on the day of the collapse. The investigation also identified a failure to ensure that the appropriate main-truss gusset-plate calculations were performed, inadequate design review, and a practice of excluding gusset plates from load-rating analyses. These are findings about I-35W, not findings about any other bridge.

The error had a concrete form and a grave outcome. The original plans specified 0.5-inch gusset plates at the U4, U10, and L11 nodes, and those plates were fabricated and installed as specified. A later Federal Highway Administration evaluation, reported by the NTSB, found that 24 plates at those nodes should have been approximately 1 inch thick to achieve acceptable demand-to-capacity ratios. The NTSB identified the inadequate capacity of the U10 plates, caused by a design error and acting under increased bridge weight plus traffic and concentrated construction loads, as the probable cause of the collapse. Thirteen people died and 145 were injured. The specific thickness finding and casualty count appear in NTSB report HAR-08/03; they do not establish that a different bridge has the same defect.

Source: [NTSB, Collapse of I-35W Highway Bridge](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/HWY07MH024.aspx).

## The transferable lesson

A completed review establishes that a process took place; it does not, by itself, establish that every safety-critical component was included in the calculation. For a structural change or a load-sensitive decision, identify the components and assumptions on which the conclusion depends, then trace each one to the relevant demand, capacity, and calculation record. A signature or a general statement of compliance cannot close a gap in that trace.

This is a review method inferred from the I-35W findings. It is not a substitute for a project-specific structural analysis or an independent engineer's judgment.

## Evidence worksheet for a staged change

Consider a hypothetical existing steel-truss bridge with a proposed deck overlay and temporary construction stockpiles. The example supplies no drawings, measured capacities, load schedule, or calculation book, so no conclusion about its adequacy follows from this page. A useful first review records four linked views of the evidence:

1. **Change and load view.** Separate existing conditions, construction staging, and the completed condition. For each stage, identify the proposed added weight or concentrated load, its location, and the record supporting its magnitude.
2. **Component view.** Trace each material load path through deck, members, connections, gusset plates, and supports as applicable. For each potentially controlling component, name the calculation that compares demand with capacity under the relevant stage. Mark a missing calculation as an open question rather than treating a review stamp as proof.
3. **Assumption view.** For each calculation, record the drawing revision, geometry, material properties, connection details, and load combination on which it relies. Check whether a later modification or a temporary load lies outside those assumptions.
4. **Reverse coverage view.** Start again from each load case and ask which components receive that load, then compare that list with the component view. Report each mismatch separately; a component can be absent from one load case even when both lists appear complete in isolation.

The two coverage views serve different checks. A compact review record can show one row per material component and a second row per load case, each with its source, calculation reference, status, and unresolved question. A short reconciliation paragraph for every mismatch makes the chain from evidence to provisional conclusion auditable. Repeating the check after a new calculation arrives is useful only for the rows its assumptions or results affect.

## Decision and stopping point

With only a change summary and a signed approval sheet, the defensible conclusion is provisional: obtain the relevant design and load-rating calculations, including the safety-critical connections and gusset plates where they may govern, plus the current load and staging assumptions. Seek independent technical assessment where a material assumption remains unsupported, conflicts with the records, or was outside the earlier review. Stop expanding the inquiry once the material assumptions and controlling components are adequately supported for the decision and no unresolved evidence gap could reasonably change it.

**Reusable review rule:** For a load-sensitive change, trace each material assumption and safety-critical component to the calculation that covers it. Resolve unsupported or out-of-scope assumptions with the underlying records and independent assessment where needed. A review can close when remaining gaps cannot reasonably change the decision.
