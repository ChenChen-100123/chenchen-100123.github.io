# Structural Review Standard: Checking What the Calculation Actually Covers

**Purpose:** This is a standing review practice for future structural-change and load-sensitive decisions. It is written to be reduced to a short, source-attributed entry in the workspace's MEMORY.md for use in later sessions. The I-35W material below is context for the rule; do not save the full document as a transcript.

## The rule, in brief

When reviewing a structural change or a load-sensitive decision:

1. **Identify material dependencies.** Name the components and assumptions on which the conclusion actually rests.
2. **Verify coverage.** Confirm that each safety-critical component was included in the relevant capacity or design calculation. Completing a standard review does not, by itself, establish that a given component was covered.
3. **Resolve gaps.** If a critical assumption is unsupported, inconsistent with the records, or outside the existing assessment, consult the relevant design records and seek an independent technical assessment where needed.
4. **Stop at sufficiency.** End the investigation once the material assumptions are adequately supported for the decision and no unresolved evidence gap could reasonably change the conclusion.

## The I-35W case as context

In 2007, the I-35W bridge in Minneapolis collapsed. The NTSB investigation identified inadequate gusset-plate capacity resulting from a design error, together with deficiencies in the relevant calculations and design review. Later bridge changes and the construction loads present at the time also formed part of the circumstances.

The case illustrates a distinction at the heart of the rule: confirming that a familiar review process was performed is not the same as confirming that a particular safety-critical component was covered by the underlying calculation. Apply the rule as a review method, not as a substitute for case-specific engineering analysis or independent professional judgment.

Source: [NTSB I-35W investigation](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/HWY07MH024.aspx)

## Simulated record excerpt (illustrative only)

*The following R-17 entries are simulated for purposes of this example and are not drawn from the NTSB record.*

- **R-17.1 (simulated):** A capacity check was documented as "complete" using a prior scope statement that did not list a modified connection detail.
- **R-17.2 (simulated):** A reviewer noted the omission; the record was reopened and the detail added to the calculation set.
- **R-17.3 (simulated):** Because the added component's assumption was unsupported by existing records, the design file was consulted and an independent check was obtained before the conclusion was adopted.
- **R-17.4 (simulated):** Review closed when no unresolved evidence gap remained that could change the decision.

## Concise memory entry

Source: NTSB I-35W bridge case. For future structural-change or load-sensitive reviews: identify the material components and assumptions; confirm that safety-critical components were included in the underlying capacity/design calculations; where a critical assumption lacks support or falls outside the existing assessment, examine design records and seek independent technical assessment as needed; stop once material assumptions are adequately supported and no unresolved evidence gap could change the decision.
